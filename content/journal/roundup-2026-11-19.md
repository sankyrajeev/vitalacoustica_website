## Claypaky's Cyclop L targets long-throw stadium work

Claypaky has unveiled the Cyclop L, a 1,300W white LED profile with a 270 mm front lens, a 2.8° to 45° zoom and a quoted output of over 50,000 lumens. It adds linear CMY and CTO, a 16-bit four-blade framing system, two gobo wheels and two prisms, all in an IP66 body with active dehumidification. A built-in camera connection with an optional NDI accessory positions it as a base for remote follow-spot systems, aimed at stadium and arena tours, large festivals, TV studios and permanent installations.

[Read more at PLSN](https://plsn.com/newsroom/product-news/claypaky-unveils-cyclop-l/)

## d'strict opens Arte Museum Nagoya with 114 Barco projectors

Korean studio d'strict has opened its first Arte Museum in Japan, in Nagoya, with 17 immersive spaces themed on "eternal nature". The venue runs 114 Barco projectors, 78 G-series and 36 I600 units, with short-throw lenses, showing content drawn from Japanese seasons and traditions such as cherry blossom, shrines and festivals. The Arte Museum series began in Jeju in 2020, and eight more have since opened in Asia, the US and the UAE, with Barco as d'strict's projector supplier.

[Read more at AV Magazine](https://www.avinteractive.com/territories-news/apac/dstrict-opens-its-first-immersive-arte-museum-in-japan-18-11-2026/)

## AES expands its loudness website to cover dialogue intelligibility

The Audio Engineering Society's committee on broadcast and online delivery has added dialogue intelligibility material to its loudness website, building on its report on improving intelligibility in media. The site summarises the relevant AES technical documents and the AES71 and AES77 recommendations, and adds a glossary that includes hearing-science terms. With caption and subtitle use said to be "approaching 50% of viewing", it is a practical reference for anyone mixing speech for broadcast or streaming.

[Read more at TV Tech](https://www.tvtechnology.com/standards/aes-adds-dialogue-intelligibility-to-loudness-website)

## How Jelly Roll's FOH engineer runs an SSL Live L550 Plus

FOH engineer Ron Gardner mixed Jelly Roll's 2026 tour on an SSL Live L550 Plus, chosen after a four-console comparison at Sound Image and a test at Stagecoach. The show runs about 90 inputs through MADI stage racks, with Revival 4000 channel strips on lead and guest vocals and sidechain compression helping control feedback when the singer moves onto the catwalk. Gardner credits the console's EQ, summing groups and low latency, and says he still prefers to "mix organically" rather than lean on snapshots.
