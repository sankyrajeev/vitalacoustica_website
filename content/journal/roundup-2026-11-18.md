## Planar expected to light the exterior of a smaller Sphere in Maryland

Planar has been named as the expected supplier of LED nodes for the external Exosphere display on the second US Sphere, planned for National Harbor, Maryland. A lighting report submitted to local planners gives the nodes a maximum of 5,217 nits at 192.8 mm spacing, lower than the Las Vegas venue, with the site closer to homes and a historic building. The venue is proposed at 92 metres tall with 6,000 capacity, smaller than Las Vegas on both counts, and the county planning board has given it preliminary approval.

## Star Wars heads to Cosm's 12K+ LED domes

Cosm will show Star Wars: A New Hope in its 12K+ LED domes across its four US venues from March, marking the film's 50th anniversary. The film plays in its original 2.39 aspect ratio while surrounding visuals change to show locations and scenes from the saga, developed with Disney, Lucasfilm, Industrial Light & Magic and MakeMake. It is Cosm's sixth "shared reality" title and part of a multi-year, multi-film partnership with Disney, with tickets due on sale early next year.

[Read more at AV Magazine](https://www.avinteractive.com/news/display-content/star-wars-to-launch-as-shared-reality-cinematic-experience-17-11-2026/)

## One Dante network for Bangkok's Dusit Central Park

Mahajak and Atkrist have installed HARMAN Professional systems across Dusit Central Park in Bangkok, covering a five-star hotel, residences, retail, offices and a rooftop park. A Dante network links the spaces while letting each run independently, with JBL loudspeakers, Crown amplification, BSS processing, Soundcraft consoles in the ballrooms and AMX handling control and 4K video distribution. The project won the Hospitality Project Award at the 2026 Inavate APAC Awards, a useful reference for integrators working on large mixed-use sites in the region.

## Foo Fighters toast monitor engineer Ian Beveridge on his last show

Ian Beveridge, the Foo Fighters' long-serving monitor engineer, retired after the band's show at Coopers Stadium in Adelaide, which closed the 2026 leg of their world tour. Dave Grohl brought him onstage for a toast, saying he had played only a handful of shows without him in 36 years; Beveridge's earlier credits include Green Day, Weezer and Hole. It is a rare public tribute to the role monitor engineers play behind the scenes.
