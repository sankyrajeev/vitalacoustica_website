## Nine cameras and 12G fibre for a 4K Handel opera

For The English Concert's "Handel for All" project, which records three or four London concerts a year in 4K and releases them free online, oXyFire Media captured a live performance of the opera Scipione with nine cameras. With the gallery 130 metres away on a lower floor, the team ran two 12G-capable fibre links to a stage box and fed a portable unit built around a Blackmagic ATEM Constellation 8K, working in 4K at 25p. A score reader called shots from the music, and recordings were handed over at the end of each section so editing could start straight away.

## Jupiter Systems closes, leaving video wall users without support

Video wall processor maker Jupiter Systems stopped trading on 23 November, telling customers it can no longer provide technical support or warranty service. A US presidential order had required its Chinese owner, Suirui Group, to sell the company on national security grounds, and a court-appointed receiver's search for a non-Chinese buyer did not produce a deal. Its liabilities will now go through a court-supervised claims process, leaving operators of Jupiter processors and visualisation systems to plan support themselves.

## Sound Radix adds three plug-ins in Radical Bundle 5

Sound Radix has added Powair 2, MuteØmatic Studio and the Radical1 synth to its Radical Bundle. Powair 2 levels by perceived loudness and adds a true-peak limiter, 26 loudness delivery targets including EBU R128 and ATSC A/85, and a zero-latency Live mode, while MuteØmatic Studio turns the company's free talkback-muting tool into a talkback and cue system with a phone companion app. The bundle is $699, with launch discounts until 7 December.
