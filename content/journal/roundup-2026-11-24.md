## Glasgow City Chambers gets a permanent projection mapping system

Glasgow has installed what is described as "one of the largest permanent" projection mapping set-ups in Europe on George Square, using 12 Panasonic projectors in four decorative metal enclosures linked by fibre to a VYV server. The council says owning the rig replaces renting projectors for each civic event. It opened on 19 November with a Christmas show by Funktioncreep that puts more than 300,000 lumens across the 72-metre façade, running every 20 minutes each evening until 3 January.

## Brightline's MOD2 brings its modular LED fixture to higher grids

Brightline Lighting has introduced the MOD2, a two-module version of its MOD Series studio LED fixture aimed at grids of 12 feet or higher. Each 32-watt module can be angled through plus or minus 90 degrees with spot or flood optics, with a CRI above 97, fixed or variable white options and bidirectional DMX RDM for status and temperature reporting. An optional rack-mounted driver supplies up to 600 watts for low-voltage control, and MOD3 and MOD4 versions are to follow.

[Read more at TV Tech](https://www.tvtechnology.com/production/brightline-lighting-unveils-mod2-light)

## Jungle's first North American arena tour on d&b KSL

Jungle's first North American arena tour is running a d&b audiotechnik KSL system supplied by Worley Sound, with SL-SUBs, across arenas, armouries and theatres. FOH engineer Antoine Richard has mixed the band since 2013, and systems engineer Shane Thomas uses ArrayCalc to scale the hangs from 12 to 20 boxes as rooms change. The system's cardioid behaviour helps keep stage spill out of the band's many open vocal mics.

[Read more at ProSoundWeb](https://www.prosoundweb.com/jungle-brings-sunshine-to-north-america-with-db-audiotechnik/)

## 5 Seconds of Summer tour with DiGiCo Quantum338 at both positions

5 Seconds of Summer are using DiGiCo Quantum338 consoles at FOH and monitors on their world tour, with Adlib supplying two complete systems, one for Europe and one for the US, and four SD-Racks. FOH engineer Sebastien Teti built one show file to cover arenas, fly dates and festivals, leaning on the console's onboard processing, while monitor engineer Mike Platt built his from scratch. The console's availability through rental companies worldwide is cited as a reason it suits fly dates.
